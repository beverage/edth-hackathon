# Known gaps and risks

The largest untested risks are real Ukrainian speech and mixed-language clips; the rest are known limits. Add a gap when you find one; remove it, with a decision line, when it is fixed.

- **Mixed languages in one clip** (measured 21 Nov, [voices.md](voices.md#mixed-languages)): with synthetic voices, 14 of 18 two-language clips kept both halves. Ukrainian then French failed 3 of 3 in the Kyiv area: the French came back spelled in Cyrillic or replaced by made-up Ukrainian ("Je suis devant la mairie" as "Я сидів на мурі"). One English half before Ukrainian was dropped without a trace. The area's place names, sent as hints, pull the clip towards the area's language: the same clip sent in the Vincennes area kept its French. Pinning the language is not possible, Mistral refuses it with diarization [106]; since [190] a speaker whose button is not the area's language gets phrases in their own language as hints, which kept French with Ukrainian in 4 of 6 clips on the Kyiv map, against 0 of 6 before.
- **One place per report:** "the corner of A and B", "between A and B" and "at A, going to B" keep only one place. A crossing is found from one street named and the street the unit is on [117]; with no street known, "the corner of A and B" asks among the crossings of A. In "the pharmacy on rue de Fontenay" the street does not narrow the choice.
- **Counting blocks** [184]: crossings are put in order by straight-line distance, so on a street that bends a count can skip a crossing. A count starts only from the unit or from a street, not from a place ("two blocks east of the church" asks how far). A lane that ends at the street counts in the "every street" count, so the app asks more often in old centres. The French and Ukrainian block words and questions have not been checked by a native speaker, and the Ukrainian question leaves the street out, since its name would need a case ending.
- **Only a short spoken answer is heard** [251]: "two", "the metro station" or "yes" to one choice is shown picked on the command post, and the operator confirms it [178, 208]. Anything longer, and "say again", "disregard" or "none", is read as a new report, and that report closes the question, unless it is about a track [241]. The reader is checked on typed replies only, none through speech-to-text, and its French and Ukrainian words were written without a native speaker. For a track this loses the track: "by the church" in reply to "Where is it?" is read as being about the speaker, and the track's question closes with the track not placed (from reading the code, not run). The model reply reader is measured, not built ([ask-back.md](ask-back.md)).
- **Radio reports have not met real radio audio** [202]. The call-sign reader is measured on hand-written transcripts, and radio reports were checked typed. A held report keeps its recording on the coordinator, readable by anyone who can reach it, like the logs (see No auth below). More in [callsigns.md](callsigns.md#not-done).
- **A call sign at the end** [237] is read as the sender only after a written pause, so it leans on speech-to-text's commas: said with no pause, the report waits in the Decisions list. Speaking to a unit looks the same as signing off: "Meet me at the church, Bravo 2" is read as from BRAVO 2. Checked on hand-written transcripts and synthetic voices, not on real speech ([callsigns.md](callsigns.md#a-call-sign-at-the-end)).
- **Misreadings found by the stage eval, with the offline model** (22 Nov, [stage-test-plan.md](hackathon/stage-test-plan.md#pass-1-typed)). A sighting with no word for whose it is can be read as the speaker moving: "Alpha 1, vehicle, bearing 60 degrees, 300 metres" and "contact at 2 o'clock, 300 metres" both were, where "unknown vehicle, bearing 060, 300 metres" was read right. The far guard held both, but after a wait they would move the unit. "Drone 200 metres north of grid 586 101" was read as about a Track 2 that did not exist. "At the station" was not placed, and "Vincennes train station" asked which one. "At the castle" asks castle or metro station, since both are named Château de Vincennes. A spoken answer to a question ("the metro station") asks again; only the operator's tap answers.
- **"The next crossing" outside a mission:** the phone does not send the street it is on or the way it is going, so with no mission the coordinator asks which crossing every time.
- **No shops, cafés, restaurants, banks or house numbers** in the gazetteer ([gazetteer.md](gazetteer.md#how-it-is-built)). Stops, fuel stations, pharmacies, hotels, water and named buildings are in.
- **A name shared by a hotel and a landmark goes to the nearest one.** From Maidan, "Я біля Дніпра" picks Hotel Dnipro, 250 m away, not the river; before hotels were in, it picked the Dnipro metro station, 2.6 km away. Saying "the river" picks the river. The model's guess of a category is not used unless the word was said [15].
- **A bridge with no name is not a place.** None of Izium's 12 road bridges has a name in OpenStreetMap, so «Я біля мосту» is not placed; the same holds for the other towns counted near the front. Other limits the Izium build showed are in [gazetteer.md](gazetteer.md#izium-added-saturday-21-november): «біля вокзалу» is read as the wrong kind of place, and English names barely exist there.
- **A unit placed by hand, limits** [243]: there is no undo; the operator places it again. The place the app had stays in the trail, so a correction draws a line to a place the unit never was. The map label does not say a place came from the command post; the units list and the feed do. A unit's marker under another unit's numbered choice is hard to see. Checked in a desktop browser with typed reports, not in a replay.
- **Tracks, not built yet:** a question when a new sighting is close to an old track (each unnumbered sighting opens a new track), "Track 3 is gone" said by a unit, and range rings for a system [234]. The command post can mark a track gone by hand [177].
- **Recon requests and tasking, limits** [234]: the request words are read from lists, in English, French, Ukrainian and Russian; a way of asking not listed ("get me eyes on that", «киньте пташку») is read as a report, so its words reach the model. The French, Ukrainian and Russian words were written without a native speaker. There is no "send it to me": "send the drone to my position" is a request with no track, and the operator picks one. A send does not move the drone's marker, and nothing tells the unit that a drone is coming: the phone hears nothing back [208]. A request in a held radio report is read only when the operator gives the report to a unit. A unit's kind is set by hand at the command post for each mission; it is not kept from one mission to the next, and only drones and drone teams can be sent: no other friendly unit [235]. Checked with typed reports through the local model, on the command post in a desktop browser; not with speech or a real phone.
- **The Decisions card over the map, limits** [258]: once the operator has moved the map by hand, a decision that opens by itself does not move it, so when both sides of the map hold some of its choices the card covers one or more until the decision is opened. A dragged card can cover the zoom buttons and the map tools. On a phone the card takes up to 45% of the map. Not checked at 1700 px and up.
- **The command post's lanes, limits** [259]: on a touch screen a strip cannot be dragged (no long-press yet); Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ need a keyboard. The order, sort, groups and filter are kept in one browser: another command post, or this one in a private window, has its own. Units have no sort of their own, only an order by hand. The open panel of chips pushes the tracks down, so on a short screen it hides most of them until it is closed. A filter hides tracks from the list, never from the map.
- **The Decisions list, limits** [240]: checked in a desktop browser with 30 call signs and 37 typed radio reports, not yet with a live operator under time pressure. Keys reach the first nine options only; a tenth unit for a held report is clicked. A recon request has no keys: its buttons are clicked. The list is one per command post: two people at two command posts see the same list and can pick the same decision, and the first answer stands. A reply on the net with no call sign still goes only to a unit with a question with choices [204]; a question with no choices does not count.
- **A phone that answers after the command post** keeps its own choice on its screen until its next report. The coordinator keeps the command post's answer, which came first, and the phone's next report starts from there [178]. The transmitter ignores the reply's `by`.
- **A placed track's name** comes from the nearest place: within 30 m its name, else "N m east of" a landmark within 200 m, or the nearest street. A landmark's distance is from its middle, so a large one can read further than the street next to the point [177].
- **Tracks, limits of what is built:** a negation is not understood, so "not hostile" marks a track hostile [148]. A count the model misses is not read from the words ("two suspicious vehicles" came back once with no count). Only the newest question is shown on a phone, so two track questions in a row hide the first. "Heading towards the bridge" gives no point and the app asks, even for a track that already has a place [149].
- **Compass words for a track are read from word lists** [186]: English, French, Ukrainian and Russian. "NE" or "S" said as letters, "à l'orient" and other forms not listed are not found; then the model's reading stands, except a heading beside a bearing or a clock direction, which is dropped [246]. "Coming from the north" gives no heading, though it means moving south. The eval's outcome for a dead-reckoned track checks only that it was placed, not which way, so a swap is seen only in the `direction` and `track_heading` fields.
- **Clock directions are read on the map, 12 o'clock north, unless the speaker says which way they face** [198, 224]. By radio habit a clock is counted from the way the speaker faces; the app knows that way only when it is said ("facing north", "face au nord", "дивлюсь на північ"), so "contact at 3 o'clock" from someone walking north-east goes east, not south-east. The phone's compass is not used. "At 3 o'clock" said as a time of day is read as a direction too. Only "o'clock", "à … heures" and "на … години/часа" are found, not "half past four" or "at 3" alone. The French, Ukrainian and Russian forms were written without a native speaker. The map label gives the nearest of eight compass points ("north-east" for 2 o'clock), though the pin is on the clock's own 60°.
- **The French and Ukrainian words for tracks are unchecked:** "Piste 3" and "Об'єкт 3", the kinds and the adjectives were written without a native speaker. The Ukrainian voice cannot say digits, so the system voice reads the number [44]. The command post's own words for tracks are English only.
- **Tracks are checked with typed reports and synthetic voices only:** in a desktop browser at 1440 × 900, through hosted Mistral and the local 3B model, and the tracks script's 11 clips through the coordinator. On 21 Nov the phone screens for a track were opened in the iPhone simulator, with ALPHA 1's clips sent from the transmitter, twice ([tracks.md](tracks.md#on-the-phone)). Not with a real phone, a person's voice, or a projector.
- **A track's symbol drawn off its point** ([tracks.md](tracks.md#when-symbols-would-overlap)) [167]: where a symbol would hide a unit or another track, it is drawn nearby with a line back. In a crowded spot it can end up well away from its point, and the line can cross another label. Label sizes are estimated from the letters, not measured.
- **A short mission on a long day is thin on the replay bar** [206]: the bar holds the whole day, so an 8-minute mission among 10 hours of missions is a few pixels wide and a drag inside it moves a minute a pixel. Play, the arrow keys and Page Up and Page Down still reach every moment. There is no zoom onto one mission.
- **Other voices in a clip:** the app cannot tell whose voice is whose; when two voices name different places it asks [154], unless all but one put the speaker out of reach of the last position [188]. It finds voices where Whisper on the phone marks them, and where Mistral's diarization labels a change of speaker [175]; Mistral's labels are not yet checked on real clips ([voices.md](voices.md)).
- **Whispered reports are untested:** whether Whisper and Voice Isolation keep a whisper, and whether loudness and voicing tell the speaker from other voices on real recordings. Test clips: [voices.md](voices.md#test-clips-155) [155, 156].
- **Grid references are checked with typed words only** [223]: MGRS grids ("grid DQ 586 101", "Delta Quebec 586 101") are read in code and match the `mgrs` library to a metre, but no spoken grid has gone through speech-to-text yet, and how the transcriber writes "five eight six" (digits or words) is not known; both are read. Latitude and longitude said out loud are not parsed. The map page shows the grid under the pointer, its lines, a ruler and "go to grid" [231, 232]. The command post writes each position's six-figure grid after it, takes a grid in its place search, and keeps the same tools behind one "Map tools" button [245]. None of it has been tried on a phone, a touch screen or a projector, and the command post's grids were looked at with three units, not thirty. The grid lines are one a kilometre: at the zoom of a foot patrol there may be none on screen.
- **The local model can pin a place nobody named** ([evals/README.md](../evals/README.md#open), 21 Nov): Ministral 3 3B writes "Bérault", a name from the prompt's examples, for "Two blocks past the church" and "Je suis derrière l'hôtel de ville". Two places in one report, which pinned the place being left, is fixed on hosted Mistral; the local model still misreads "On rue de Fontenay, going to the pharmacy".
- **Other extraction misses, through hosted Mistral** ([evals/README.md](../evals/README.md), 21 Nov): "outside" is read as near; "Copy that, moving out" becomes a movement. None of these pins a wrong place. The local 3B model fails 15 of 65 cases every time, also with no wrong pin.
- **Real Ukrainian speech is untested;** all Ukrainian tests used a synthetic voice. Short clips sometimes came back misspelled, though the place still resolved.
- **The map with no internet is fetched on each computer, one area a run** [239] (22 Nov): `npm run basemap -- vincennes`, then `kyiv`, then `izium`, with internet, before starting the coordinator. The copy is not in git and is as old as the day it was fetched. Where an area was not fetched, a mission there in local mode has little or nothing under its units; where nothing was fetched, the units draw on a plain background. Tiles stop at zoom 14, as OpenFreeMap's do. Outside an area the map gets coarser, then empty. Checked in a desktop browser that refused other hosts, not yet on the stage Mac with its Wi-Fi off. The coordinator must be restarted after a fetch: `npm start` lists the files it serves when it starts.
- **Some small map icons are missing,** hosted and saved alike: the "liberty" style names icons its icon sheet does not hold (gate, bollard, atm and others), so those points show a name and no icon.
- **Speech-to-text on the coordinator in offline mode** [195] is checked with the 29 synthetic scenario clips only ([stt-comparison.md](stt-comparison.md#offline-on-the-coordinator)). A spoken report takes 3 to 5 s on the M1 Pro, close to the 4 s target, and longer while something else uses the GPU. Whisper does not tell speakers apart, so a clip with two voices is read as one. Without the device's language it writes Ukrainian as Russian; the web page and the transmitter always send one. "Bérault" said by an English voice came back "Bayrold" with no prompt; with the short prompt of decision 247 it comes back right on one voice of two. The prompt is checked on one real English voice, one clip, and the synthetic clips: French and Ukrainian speakers are not tested, and Kyiv and Izium get no prompt. Real-voice clips of the team are to come.
- **The offline model reads a report differently without its full stop** (21 Nov): "Je suis à la mairie de Vincennes" is ambiguous without one and placed with one, and "Suspicious vehicle near the church" stops being a track. Both every time. The Whisper prompt ends in a full stop so transcripts keep theirs [247]; a report typed without one still meets this.
- **Accents are checked with synthetic voices only** (21 Nov): one teammate's American "Vincennes" did not resolve. Through server speech-to-text, 14 clips in four American macOS voices all came back as "Vincennes", even when spelled to sound American ("Vin Senz station", "Shatto dee Vin Senz"), because the area's place names are sent as hints. So the accent is not yet shown to break it. Not checked: the teammate's own voice, and Whisper on the phone, whose hints are weaker [73]. To see what a voice turns into, run the coordinator with `KEEP_CLIPS=1`: each clip and its transcript are kept in `.data/clips/`. Saying the type word ("Vincennes station", "the RER") turns most misheard names into a question rather than a pin; "Vincent station", above, is the exception.
- **Language tagging, for clients that do not send `language`:** the web page and the transmitter always send it [106]. Without it, an English sentence with a French street name was tagged as French, so a follow-up question would have been in French. A report in Latin letters is never tagged Ukrainian unless speech-to-text said so [104].
- **A pinned language that is wrong is not caught:** a phone left on Українська while someone speaks English makes Whisper write the English in Cyrillic, and the place is not found [106].
- **Mistral rate limits and outages** are tried again twice, waiting at most 2 s each [191]; a timeout is not, so one slow call can still fail the report. Most calls take under a second, but one took 8.3 s against a 10 s timeout.
- **Interface text is English only;** only the question and place names follow the speaker's language [39].
- **No auth;** the rate limit is per server instance and is not a security control. Anyone who can reach the coordinator can open, end or delete a mission, or join one under any free call sign.
- **A phone that switches coordinator** forgets the mission it was on, and picks again on the new one [208].
- **The phone's own search of the network, limits** [248]: iPhone only; Android finds the coordinator through USB forwarding alone. An iPhone needs one rebuild to get it. Checked on 22 Nov on one iPhone 13 mini, with the Mac on that phone's hotspot and the cable in: the search found three coordinators within seconds and the phone reached the fixed one. Following the connected coordinator to a new address is checked in unit tests, not live. A coordinator or dev server still on the old advert code (any branch without this change) can rename the Mac again when it starts; the phone finds the coordinator anyway, but Metro and a build made before the rename keep the old name. A coordinator started before this change keeps advertising the addresses it had when it started, such as the venue Wi-Fi's, until it restarts.
- **The phone hears nothing back** [208]: it shows only that a report was sent or held. The operator's question and calls do not reach it, as they would over a radio.
- **No hosted copy:** the live site was paused on 23 November [261]. Everything runs on a computer of your own.
- **The native app's choice list does not show the town** of two same-name streets; the spoken question does ([gazetteer.md](gazetteer.md#same-name-streets)).
